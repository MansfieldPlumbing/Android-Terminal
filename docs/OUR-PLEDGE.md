# Our Pledge

Terminal is your computer.

What you type, build, serve, edit and explore belongs to you. Mansfield Plumbing does not need a copy of it, a profile of you, or a dashboard measuring how you use your own machine.

## We collect nothing

Terminal has:

- no analytics;
- no telemetry;
- no advertising;
- no tracking pixels;
- no behavioral profiling or fingerprinting;
- no Mansfield Plumbing user database;
- no cloud copy of your commands, files, scripts, sessions or identity;
- no crash upload silently carrying terminal contents;
- no sale, rental or sharing of personal data—because we do not collect it.

There is no Mansfield Plumbing account to build a dossier around.

## Your work stays on your device

Terminal's history, settings, scripts, credentials and identity mappings remain local unless you deliberately export, transmit or serve them.

Starting a server, opening a listener, sharing a file or connecting a remote client is an explicit act. Terminal must show what is exposed, where it is exposed and how to stop it. Network capability is never permission to quietly phone home.

## Optional sign-in is only a doorway

If you choose remote sign-in, `mansfieldplumbing.dev` is a static portal to Google or Microsoft. It has no Mansfield Plumbing identity backend, user database, analytics or persistent session store.

The portal is optional. Terminal's local shell, files, scripts, capabilities and BOS environment require no Mansfield Plumbing account, Google account, Microsoft account or Internet connection.

Nothing prevents an owner from using a different, explicitly configured entrance to services running on their own device. Mansfield Plumbing tends only the `mansfieldplumbing.dev` portal and its documented Google/Microsoft handoff. An owner-managed proxy, private network, certificate authority or authentication package remains under that owner's control and is never silently routed through Mansfield Plumbing.

Google or Microsoft necessarily processes the sign-in you ask that provider to perform under its own terms. Terminal requests the minimum identity scope and retains only the minimum provider identity key locally when needed to recognize your session. Mansfield Plumbing does not receive or store that identity.

Internet infrastructure such as DNS, hosting providers and networks may maintain operational logs outside Terminal's control. We will describe those boundaries honestly rather than pretending the Internet produces no traces.

## Permission must have a purpose

Terminal will not treat a manifest permission as consent.

Sensitive capabilities must be:

- explained before use;
- requested only when a feature needs them;
- scoped as narrowly as the platform permits;
- visible while active;
- revocable by the user;
- released when their work is done.

Signing in proves identity. It does not grant access to your files, shell, camera, network listeners or Android capabilities. Those remain separate decisions under your control.

## You can verify the promise

Terminal's source, permission boundaries and release receipts are intended to be inspectable. Security does not rest on this memo alone. It rests on narrow platform adapters, explicit capability grants, isolated worker generations and receipts that fail when the implementation violates the promise.

## If this ever changes

We will not quietly weaken this pledge through an update, settings default or revised legal paragraph.

Any future feature that genuinely requires Mansfield Plumbing to receive user data must be:

1. optional;
2. explained before collection;
3. separately consented to;
4. documented before release;
5. removable without taking away the local Terminal you already have.

We would rather remove a feature than hide its cost from you.

**No surveillance. No advertising. No data business. Your machine remains yours.**
